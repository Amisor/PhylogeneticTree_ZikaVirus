# PhylogeneticTree_ZikaVirus

A patient returns from a trip to Brazil with fever, joint pain, rash and headache, and only one short viral sequence could be recovered from their sample. The goal of this repository is to identify the likely cause of the infection and trace its probable origin using phylogenetics. I identified the virus with NCBI BLAST, retrieved related sequences from GenBank, aligned them, built Neighbor-Joining phylogenetic trees, and coloured each strain by its country of origin.

## Results

* **Pathogen:** the top NCBI BLAST match was a **Zika virus** envelope protein (E) gene sequence (AF372422.1).
* **Origin:** in the tree built from 30 sequences ("Zika virus envelope protein (E) gene"), 23 strains came from Latin America (Brazil, Cuba, Mexico and Ecuador), 6 from Asia (India and Thailand) and 1 had an unknown origin. Strains clustered by country, and the top BLAST hit clustered more closely with Latin American strains, including Brazilian ones, than with Asian strains. This is consistent with an infection acquired in Brazil.
* **Lineages:** the broader tree built from 146 sequences ("Zika virus envelope protein") also included African strains, in line with the known African and Asian lineages of Zika virus.
* **Original Antigenic Sin:** Zika and Dengue viruses are closely related flaviviruses, so a previous Dengue infection can produce cross-reactive antibodies with low neutralizing potential against Zika, which may enhance the infection (antibody-dependent enhancement).

## Pipeline

1. **Identify the pathogen** with NCBI BLAST.
2. **Retrieve sequences** from GenBank (sequence length restricted to 1–1000 nucleotides): 30 sequences from an exact query and 146 from a broader one.
3. **Align** the sequences with MUSCLE.
4. **Build the trees** with Simple Phylogeny (Neighbor-Joining).
5. **Colour the trees** by country of origin in iTOL, using the metadata generated by the R scripts in `src`.
